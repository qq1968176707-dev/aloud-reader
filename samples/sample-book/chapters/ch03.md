# Chapter 3 · Reading aloud in English

This chapter exists so you can hear the reader switch languages without touching a setting.

The language of every segment is detected from the segment itself, not from the book. A Chinese book that quotes an English sentence will speak that sentence with an English voice, and then go straight back to the Chinese one. The rule is simple: count the CJK characters, count the Latin words, and let the larger side win.

## What the highlight is actually doing

The active line is painted with the CSS Custom Highlight API. That matters more than it sounds. The highlight is a *Range*, not a wrapper element, so nothing in the document is modified to paint it. No reflow, no invalidated selection, no fighting with overlapping annotations, and — the part you can feel — no flicker when the window is resized while the audio keeps playing.

When the speech engine reports word boundaries, a second, narrower Range is painted inside the first one. The engine gives a character index into the sentence it is speaking; the reader adds the sentence's start offset and gets a position in the paragraph. Two additions, no guessing.

## Three things worth trying

- Set the speed to 1.5× and notice that the word highlight keeps up.
- Turn on "read to the end of the chapter and stop", then let it run to the end.
- Scroll away while it is speaking, then stop scrolling: the reader will bring the spoken line back into view on the next sentence.

The last one is the small detail that makes listening and reading at the same time bearable.
